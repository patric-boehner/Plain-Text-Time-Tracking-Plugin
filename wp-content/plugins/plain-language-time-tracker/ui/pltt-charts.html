Charts

Two types, and only two. Bar chart — a quantity over time. Meter — one value against a limit. Composition ("where the time went") stays a table with inline meters; a pie or stacked bar would be worse at the one thing you do with it, which is read exact figures per project.

The meter has a real problem: an over-budget bar currently fills the track and shows the excess in a lighter tint, which reads as remaining. And because the track caps, 249% and 500% look nearly the same.

Rules

Under or at the limit — track scales so the limit is the full width; marker sits at the right edge. Fill is blue.

Over — track scales so the total is the full width; the marker moves left to where the limit falls. Everything past it is amber. Never cap the bar; the marker's position is what communicates how far over.

No limit — hourly projects with no budget. Show the bar with no marker and no percentage, or omit the meter entirely. Do not invent a limit.

The marker is labelled with what it is — budget, 3h included — so a meter is readable without the caption underneath it.

ChangeReason
Solid tints, no hatching Diagonal hatching is noisy below about 20px wide and reads as a texture rather than a category. Three solid steps — dark green, light green, warm grey — separate cleanly at any size.
Days with nothing logged draw nothing Currently a full-height grey column, which is the visually heaviest mark on the chart and represents the absence of data. Now a 2px baseline tick — present, unmissable, weightless.
Value labels removed from bars Labels above every bar plus a y-axis says everything twice, and at 30 bars the labels collide. Axis plus hover for exact values.
Round-number axis, two gridlines 45h / 23h / 0 — 23 is an arbitrary midpoint. Use 0, a round mid, and a round top.
Sparse x labels Every fifth day for a month, every month for a year. Enough to locate yourself, not enough to become a wall of text.
Average line kept Descriptive and genuinely useful for spotting a heavy or light stretch. Label sits on the line at the right edge, on a paper-coloured chip so it never overlaps a bar.
Total in the legend row The chart's own summary — 95h 39m across 16 days — sits with the legend rather than needing a caption.

Shared rules

Rule
Series colours are fixedBillable #146b42 · non-billable #7fb094 · internal #c5c2bd · past a limit #c08f2a. Never reassigned per chart; the same colour means the same thing everywhere.
Never redOver a budget or allocation is amber. Nothing in a chart is an error state.
Absence is not a markA day with no work draws a baseline tick, never a filled column. Zero and no-data look the same because for this tool they mean the same thing.
Y axis always starts at zeroTruncating the axis exaggerates variation, which is a form of editorialising.
Every chart states its periodIn the panel header, same wording as everywhere else — · Jun 1 – Jun 30, 2026.
Granularity is a panel controlBy day / by week / by month at panel weight, right of the panel header. Never changes what the page is scoped to.
Exact values on hoverDate, total, and the split. The chart shows shape; the tooltip carries precision.
No chart without a decisionIf you can't name what someone does differently after looking at it, use a table.

Why no third chart type

"Where the time went" is the obvious candidate for a pie or stacked bar. But what you do with that panel is read exact hours and money per project, and compare a couple of them — which a table does better, with the inline meter carrying the proportion. A second visual encoding of the same data would add a thing to learn without answering a new question.
