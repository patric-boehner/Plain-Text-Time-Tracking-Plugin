Budget on the chart

The Budget consumed panel restates the scope figure and takes a full panel to do it. It comes out. The budget moves onto the bar chart, where it answers something the figure can't: when you went over, and how much work happened after.

The pace forecast is a line of text under the chart, not a burndown. It is one sentence of information and a chart would be a container for a sentence.

What the chart earns that the figure didn't

Mar 29 is the date, and the wash covers ten of sixteen weeks. That's the finding: the budget was gone in six weeks and the project ran nearly twice as long again afterwards. The figure says 249%; the chart says the estimate was wrong from the start rather than the end drifting.

The crossing rule is ink, not amber, for the same reason the meter's marker is ink — the marker is the limit, amber is the excess. Sits inside the bar, at the point in the week the total actually passed, not snapped to the week edge.

At 6h 12m a week, the 60h budget is spent around Sep 10 — 25h 48m left.

What it can't say

It can't say "and you'll still have x to go." That needs a remaining-scope estimate, which the tool doesn't store and shouldn't start guessing. The sentence is a pace extrapolation and nothing more: at this rate, the budget runs out on this date. You supply the judgment about whether the work will be done by then.

The average is the same 6h 12m already drawn on the chart as the average line, so the sentence is showing its work rather than introducing a number from nowhere.

SUPPRESSED — already over
No pace line. The crossing marker on the chart is the fact; a forecast of an event that already happened is noise.
SUPPRESSED — not enough history
Under three weeks of entries, or under 15% of budget used. A pace built on two weeks predicts a date that moves by a month each time you log.
04

Retainers get a line, not a crossing

The allocation resets.

A retainer has no single moment it went over — it goes over every month. A vertical crossing would be wrong. The allocation is a horizontal rule on the by-month chart and the part of each bar above it is amber.

Same question, different mechanism

"When did I go over" on a retainer means "which months," and the answer here is all of them — which is the argument for resizing the plan, and the same thing the average per month figure says. No pace line: a monthly period is too short to forecast, and the threshold notice on Today already covers the current month.

One thing to watch after a month of use

If the pace date bounces week to week, the rule that suppresses it is wrong, not the idea. Widen the averaging window to the last four weeks before adding anything else — and if it still bounces, drop the line rather than dressing it with a confidence range.
