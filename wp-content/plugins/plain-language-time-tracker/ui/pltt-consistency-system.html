One header, four screens

The last round tightened individual screens and made the app less consistent — five different containers for the same idea. This starts from the opposite end: one way of stating what you're looking at, what period it covers, and what it's worth, then applies it unchanged to four screens that currently each do it differently.

Labels get longer here, not shorter. Nothing is compressed. The goal is that you never have to work out what a number means or which dates it covers.

01

The pattern

Identity, period, figures. Always in that order.

Every screen that shows numbers opens with this block. The top half says what you're looking at and exactly which dates. The bottom half carries the figures — each one a full label, a value, and a line stating what it's calculated from. No bare numbers, ever.

Line 1 — what this is

The thing's own name, plus a badge naming its billing model in words a client would recognise. Same badge vocabulary on every screen.

Line 2 — what it belongs to and its terms

Parent (client, or "Own time"), then the arrangement stated in plain language: hours included, fee agreed, or rate. This is where second-guessing usually starts, so it's said up front rather than implied by a meter.

Line 3 — the period, spelled out

Always the same words, always the same place, always literal dates. Never "This Month" or "Last period" on their own. Everything below this line is inside these dates, and nothing on the screen reaches outside them.

The figures — label, value, basis

Three lines each. The basis line is the part that stops the second-guessing: it says which entries were counted and what they were multiplied by.

Why the basis line matters more than the number

"Earned this period $244.50" on its own invites the question *is that all of it, or just the overage?* — and you'd have to remember how retainers work to answer. "Overage only · 2h 43m × $90/hr" answers it in the same glance. That extra line is the whole point of this round.

02

Fixed vocabulary

One word per concept, used everywhere.

Right now the same idea has different names on different screens — Total Hours, Hours logged, Total time. And billing state is spread across three columns that mostly hold dashes, so an empty cell has to be interpreted rather than read. Both are fixed by deciding the words once.

The one figure that reaches outside the period

"Not yet billed" is money owed to you regardless of which month you're looking at, so its basis line says so explicitly. That's the rule: if a number breaks the period frame, the basis line has to admit it.

07

What this costs and what it buys

ChangeEffect
One scope block on every screenFour screens that currently open four different ways open the same way. Nothing new is calculated — the figures already exist, they're just labelled and placed identically.
Basis line under every figureTakes more vertical space than the current cards. Buys the thing you asked for: never having to work out what a number counted or what it was multiplied by.
Period stated in literal dates, one place"This Month," "Last Month," and "Last period" stop appearing as standalone labels. Every panel below the block that could be misread restates the range in its header.
One billing column, five named statesRemoves every dash in the entries table and makes "inside the allocation" readable as a deliberate state rather than missing data.
Fixed word listTime logged, client time, own time, earned, not yet billed, rate achieved. Six words, used identically everywhere, including in the invoice text you hand a client.
