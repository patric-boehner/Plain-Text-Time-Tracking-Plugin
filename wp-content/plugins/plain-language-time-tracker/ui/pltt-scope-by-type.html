Scope block per project type — report surface

The Report tab is archived-only (locked in PROJECT.md), but the scope block also heads the active project's lifetime view and filtered Entries. So each type needs a figure set that holds up both while running and once closed.

Testing each figure against one question: what decision does it inform? Anything that only creates pressure or restates another figure comes out.

No budget figure, and that's correct

An hourly project has no ceiling to measure against — that's what makes it hourly. The four figures are all it can say: how much work, what it's worth, what's owed, what's settled. Adding an effective-rate figure would be noise; the rate is fixed and already on line 2.

Same set active or archived. Nothing here needs the project to be finished to make sense.

Average per month is the load-bearing figure

It's the one that tells you something you'd act on: this plan is running at more than double its allocation every single month, which is a conversation with the client about resizing the retainer. That's the decision this view exists to inform.

5 of 5 over matters alongside the average — an average of 211% could be one wild month, but "5 of 5" says it's the shape of the engagement, not an outlier.

Why not a fourth figure

Effective rate is the obvious candidate and it's exactly wrong while active — $3,870 ÷ 28h 26m reads $136/hr today and drops every hour you work, so mid-project it's a number that only ever looks worse. PROJECT.md already rejects live EHR for this reason.

Three honest figures beat four with one that misleads. The empty slot is fine.

The archived-only figure

Once the hours stop, effective rate is a true number and the single most useful thing the project can tell you: this job paid $40/hr against a $100 target. That's what shapes whether you quote the next one like it. Retrospective, no pressure — exactly the case PROJECT.md carves out.

It's also the only figure that appears conditionally on lifecycle. Everything else is present throughout; this one waits for archive.

No money figures at all

Internal work has no rate, no bill, no budget — so three of the standard figures have nothing to show. Rather than four dashes, the block carries only what's true: how much time, what share of the whole, when you last touched it.

Share of tracked time is the one worth debating. It informs a real decision — am I spending too much unbilled — but it edges toward the self-scoring PROJECT.md warns against. Descriptive framing ("12% of tracked time") keeps it on the right side; "only 12% billable" would not. Include it cautiously, or drop to two figures if it ever reads as judgment.

The through-line

Figure 1 is always total hours — the one thing every project has. After that each type diverges by what it actually has to measure: hourly against nothing, retainer against a monthly allocation, fixed against a budget, internal against nothing but itself. No type is padded to four for symmetry, and no figure appears that the type can't honestly fill.
