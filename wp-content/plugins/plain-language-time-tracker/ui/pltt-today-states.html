Today's three states, and the status column

The log recedes as the day progresses. Capturing — full height, active, the only thing on screen. Confirming — collapsed to a peek, because you're working in the table. Recorded — read-only at reduced height, with editing available but deliberate.

Read-only by default isn't only about space: once processed, entries are derived from text that you can still change. Making the log inert until you ask for it prevents quietly editing the source of records you've already saved.

01

Status — three values

Less than I proposed before.

Unbilled and Billed are the only real billing statuses. Not charged earns a third slot because it's a decision you made that's otherwise invisible — an excluded entry and a fixed-fee entry both showing a dash is the one genuinely ambiguous case. Everything else stays a dash: in allocation and in fixed fee aren't statuses, they're facts about the project, and the project name is already in the row.

Why not name the quiet ones

I argued for In allocation and In fixed fee earlier. The case against: they repeat what the project name and the dashed Billable column already say, they add a third grey chip competing with the two that matter, and they turn a column you scan into a column you read. A dash there is accurate — there is no billing status, because nothing will ever be billed.

Two affordances, not one

Expand shows the whole log still read-only — for when you just want to reread the day, which is the common case and shouldn't risk anything. Edit log returns it to a live textarea with Save and Re-process, and is the deliberate act.

Reduced height also puts the entries table above the fold, which is what you're actually looking at once the day is captured.

05

Editing a processed log

The one place a warning belongs.

@ timestamp# tag
Cancel Re-process… Save log

Why the warning goes here and nowhere else

It's the only moment where the relationship between log and entries can surprise you. Stating it once, at the point of action, is better than a permanent note on the screen — which would become furniture within a week.

Re-process keeps its ellipsis. It's the one action that can produce duplicates, and it should confirm what it's about to do.
